## Version B: Feature Pitch

**Subject:** Story idea: The dissertation that predicted today's media crisis

Hi [Name],

Quick pitch for a feature that might resonate with your readers:

In 1986, a young PhD student at NYU wrote a dissertation arguing that professional journalism couldn't solve its deepest problems through better standards or practices—because the problems were structural. The commercial conditions enabling the press to thrive were the same conditions undermining informed public discourse.

That student was Jay Rosen, who went on to become one of the most influential critics of American journalism. The dissertation was titled "The Impossible Press."

**It's never been publicly available. Until now.**

On December 2, we're releasing the full text as part of the new Jay Rosen Internet Archive. Reading it in 2025 is striking—not because Rosen predicted specific technologies, but because he identified tensions that have only intensified.

Some passages that hit differently now:

> "Sensationalism is what happens when communication outruns the sense of community."

> "The content of public life is eclipsed by the competition for attention."

> "For any press anywhere, making things public does not a public make."

The archive also includes 849+ records of Rosen's subsequent work—36 years of articles, essays, and social media posts—making it possible to trace how his thinking evolved from dissertation to present.

Would you be interested in covering this? I can provide:
- Early access to the dissertation
- Interview with Jay Rosen
- Background on the archive's construction

Let me know if this sounds like a fit.
