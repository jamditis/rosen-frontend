# The Jay Rosen Internet Archive is Live

---

### Opening

Today, I'm excited to announce the launch of the **Jay Rosen Internet Archive**—a comprehensive, searchable collection of 36 years of journalism criticism.

The archive includes **849+ records** spanning 1986 to 2025: articles, essays, blog posts, Twitter threads, videos, and more.

And for the first time ever, it includes Jay Rosen's 1986 doctoral dissertation, **"The Impossible Press: American Journalism and the Decline of Public Life."**

**[Explore the Archive →]**

---

### What's Inside

The archive preserves Jay Rosen's complete digital record:

- **765+ web articles, essays, and blog posts** from PressThink, CJR, and dozens of other publications
- **84 newspaper clippings** from 1989-2023
- **Twitter threads and posts** preserving his social media commentary
- **YouTube lectures and interviews**
- **The complete 1986 dissertation**—never before publicly available

Every record includes:
- Full-text search
- AI-assisted categorization
- Extracted entities (people, organizations, concepts)
- Archival PDF
- Proper citation information

---

### The Dissertation

Written under Neil Postman (author of *Amusing Ourselves to Death*), Jay Rosen's dissertation asks: **Can professional journalism serve democracy?**

Written before the internet, before social media, before the current crisis of trust in media—it's remarkably prescient.
