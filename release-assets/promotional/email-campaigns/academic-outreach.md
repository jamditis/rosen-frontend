### What It Is

The archive is a comprehensive, searchable collection of Jay Rosen's journalism criticism spanning 36 years (1986-2025). It includes:

- **849+ records:** Articles, essays, blog posts, Twitter threads, and videos
- **The 1986 dissertation:** "The Impossible Press," publicly available for the first time
- **Full metadata:** Categorization, extracted entities, and relationship mapping
- **Research tools:** Citation information, archival PDFs, and data export

---

### For Research

The archive includes structured data designed for academic research:

- **5,160+ entities** (people, organizations, concepts, works, events)
- **7,499+ mapped relationships** between entities and records
- **13 key concepts** tracked across the full corpus
- **6 thematic categories** for filtering
- **Temporal analysis** across 36 years

All records include proper citation information and archival PDFs.

### The Dissertation

Jay Rosen's 1986 dissertation, "The Impossible Press: American Journalism and the Decline of Public Life," was written under Neil Postman. It provides the intellectual foundation for his subsequent four decades of journalism criticism.

The dissertation is now:
- Fully searchable
- Properly citable
- Integrated with his later work in the archive
- Available as PDF and HTML
