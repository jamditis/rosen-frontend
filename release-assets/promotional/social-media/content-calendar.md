## Quote Bank

### From the Dissertation

1. "For any press anywhere, making things public does not a public make."

2. "Providing information is not all there is to informing the public."

3. "Sensationalism is what happens when communication outruns the sense of community."

4. "Presenting 'all the news' is an impossible goal, and the press that avows it can only be an impossible press."

5. "Everything said is devoted to the struggle to be heard."

6. "Journalism must be understood not as something journalists do, but as a transaction between producers and consumers, writers and readers, the press and the public."

7. "The same technologies that bring news from distant places also expand the realm of public problems."

8. "The content of public life is eclipsed by the competition for attention."

9. "The professional attitude framed a structural conflict as a matter of personal conduct, obscuring the fundamental tension between commercial imperatives and public service."

10. "Mass communication privatizes reception—news arrives in the home, to isolated individuals—and sponsors a permanent competition for attention that shapes all public discourse."

### From Key Articles

1. "The people formerly known as the audience wish to inform media people of our existence, and of a shift in power that goes with the platform shift you've all heard about." — *The People Formerly Known as the Audience* (2006)

2. "The view from nowhere is a bid for trust that advertises the viewlessness of the one who reports." — *The View from Nowhere* (2010)

3. "In Washington, the weights placed on the scales of judgment in deciding what makes a good political story are different than in your newsroom. The story that 'works' among insiders is the winner." — *The Savvy Style* (2020)

4. "The production of innocence is a way of projecting an image of yourself as someone who would never practice deception." — *The Production of Innocence* (2013)

5. "When journalists try to describe the reality rather than report the rhetoric, they are often accused of bias. The accusation works because of the professional norm that says: we report what people say." — *Why Political Coverage is Broken* (2011)
