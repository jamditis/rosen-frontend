## General Questions

### What is the Jay Rosen Internet Archive?
The Jay Rosen Internet Archive is a searchable collection of journalism criticism, media commentary, and public engagement by Jay Rosen spanning 36 years (1986-2025). It includes articles, blog posts, social media content, videos, newspaper clippings, and his 1986 doctoral dissertation.

### Who is Jay Rosen?
Jay Rosen is a professor of journalism at New York University who has been teaching and writing about the press since 1986. He is one of the most influential critics of American journalism, known for concepts like "the view from nowhere" and "the people formerly known as the audience." He founded PressThink.org and has been a leading voice on press criticism, journalism education, and the relationship between media and democracy.

### Who created this archive?
The archive was created and is maintained by Joe Amditis at the Center for Cooperative Media at Montclair State University. The project was developed with Jay Rosen's knowledge and support.

### Is this an official project?
The archive was created independently by Joe Amditis as a preservation and research project. Jay Rosen has supported the project and provided access to materials including his 1986 dissertation. The archive is hosted by the Center for Cooperative Media.

## Using the Archive

### How do I search the archive?
The archive features a "Concept Explorer" interface with several ways to find content:

**Search:** Use the search bar to search by title, summary, or pull quote. Results update as you type.

**Filters:** Click the "Filters" button to filter by:
- **Format:** Article, Video, Twitter, etc.
- **Thematic Categories:** 6 broad subject areas
- **Key Concepts:** Jay Rosen's 13 signature concepts
- **Era:** Historical time periods
- **Publication:** Original publication source

**Sort:** Sort results by date (newest/oldest) or title (A-Z/Z-A).

**Browse:** Toggle between grid and list views. Click any card to see full details including summary, pull quote, tags, and related content.

### What content is included?
The archive includes:
- 765+ web articles, essays, and op-eds
- 84 newspaper clippings (1989-2023)
- Twitter posts and threads
- Tumblr posts
- YouTube videos and lectures
- Jay Rosen's 1986 doctoral dissertation "The Impossible Press"

### What content is NOT included?
- Private correspondence
- Unpublished manuscripts
- Course materials (unless publicly shared)
- Content behind paywalls (limited)
- Content Jay Rosen has requested be excluded

### How current is the archive?
The archive is updated periodically as new content is identified and processed. There is typically a lag of several weeks to months between when content is published and when it appears in the archive, depending on processing priorities and resources.

### Can I download content?
Yes. Each record links to an archival PDF stored in Google Drive. The dissertation is available as a complete document. Click "Read original" in any record's detail view to access the source content, or look for PDF links in the record metadata.

## Research & Citation

### How do I cite content from the archive?
Each record has citation information. For detailed citation formats (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.), see our [Citation Guide].

**Quick format:**
```
Rosen, J. (YYYY). Title. Publisher. Jay Rosen Internet Archive. [URL]
```

### Can I use archive content for academic research?
Yes. The archive is designed for research and educational use. Please cite appropriately.

### Can I use archive content for journalism?
Yes. Please attribute appropriately and link to the archive when possible.

### Is there an API?
The archive currently reads from a public Google Sheets CSV export. Researchers can access the underlying data by downloading the CSV directly. A formal API is not currently available but may be developed in the future based on demand.

### Can I export data for analysis?
Yes. The archive data is available as a CSV file that can be imported into spreadsheet software, statistical packages (R, Python pandas), or other analysis tools. The data includes all metadata fields, tags, categories, and entity information. Contact us if you need assistance accessing the data for research purposes.

## Content & Accuracy

### How accurate is the data?
The archive uses a combination of automated scraping, AI analysis, and human verification. While we strive for accuracy:
- **Publication dates:** 97% coverage; some dates are estimated
- **AI classifications:** Generally accurate but occasional errors
- **Entity extraction:** Automated with some resolution errors
- **Raw text:** Not available for all records

### I found an error. How do I report it?
Please contact us at jamditis@gmail.com with:
- The record ID or URL
- The specific error
- The correct information (if known)

### Why is some content missing?
Content may be missing due to:
- Paywalls preventing access
- Deleted or removed source content
- Technical issues during scraping
- Content not yet processed

If you know of missing content, please let us know.

### How is content categorized?
Content is categorized using:
- **6 Thematic Categories:** Broad subject areas
- **13 Key Concepts:** Jay Rosen's signature ideas
- **Tags:** Descriptive keywords
- **Entities:** People, organizations, works mentioned

Categories are assigned using AI analysis with human oversight.

## Technical Questions

### What technology powers the archive?
- **Backend:** Python with Google Cloud services
- **AI Analysis:** Google Gemini API
- **Data Storage:** Google Sheets as primary database
- **Frontend:** Vanilla JavaScript (ES6 modules), PapaParse for CSV parsing
- **Fonts:** Inter and Roboto Mono via Google Fonts
- **Icons:** Tabler Icons
- **Hosting:** Center for Cooperative Media

### Is the archive accessible?
Yes. The archive is designed to meet WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards, including:
- Screen reader compatibility with ARIA labels and live regions
- Full keyboard navigation (all controls, cards, and dialogs)
- Sufficient color contrast ratios
- Semantic HTML structure with proper headings

### What browsers are supported?
The archive works in all modern browsers including Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge. JavaScript must be enabled. The interface is responsive and works on both desktop and mobile devices.

### Is my data collected?
The archive does not require registration and does not use cookies for tracking. Standard web server logs may record IP addresses and page requests. No personal information is collected or stored. The archive uses Google Fonts, which may log font requests per Google's privacy policy.
