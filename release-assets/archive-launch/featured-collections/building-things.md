# Featured Collection: Building Things

**Status:** COMPLETE

---

## Purpose

This collection highlights Jay Rosen's work as a builder of journalism infrastructure—not just a critic of existing media, but a creator of new projects, platforms, and initiatives designed to improve how journalism serves the public.

---

## Key Projects

### PressThink (2003-present)

**What it is:** One of the earliest and most influential blogs dedicated to press criticism. PressThink has served as Rosen's primary platform for developing and testing ideas over two decades.

**Why it matters:** PressThink pioneered long-form press criticism in the blogosphere. Many of Rosen's signature concepts were first developed and refined through PressThink posts. The blog attracted a readership of journalists, academics, and citizens, creating a space for serious discourse about how the press works.

**Status:** Active

**URL:** [pressthink.org](https://pressthink.org)

**Archive coverage:** The archive includes the complete PressThink post history, representing a major portion of the collection.

---

### Studio 20 at NYU (2010-present)

**What it is:** An innovative graduate program at NYU's Arthur L. Carter Journalism Institute focused on digital journalism innovation. Rather than teaching traditional journalism skills, Studio 20 trains students to build new forms of journalism for the digital age.

**Why it matters:** Studio 20 represents Rosen's commitment to building the future of journalism through education. The program has trained hundreds of students who have gone on to leadership roles in digital media and journalism organizations.

**Status:** Active

---

### NewAssignment.Net (2006-2009)

**What it is:** An experimental platform for "open-source reporting"—a concept Rosen championed that invited the public to participate in journalism by contributing expertise, sources, and reporting assistance.

**Why it matters:** NewAssignment.Net was an early experiment in participatory journalism, testing ideas about how to involve "the people formerly known as the audience" in the news production process. While the project itself ended, the concepts it tested influenced subsequent developments in collaborative and citizen journalism.

**Status:** Concluded

---

### Assignment Zero (2007)

**What it is:** A collaborative reporting project launched by NewAssignment.Net in partnership with Wired. Assignment Zero attempted to crowdsource a large-scale investigative report using contributions from hundreds of volunteers.

**Why it matters:** Assignment Zero was one of the most ambitious early experiments in crowdsourced journalism. While the project faced significant challenges (documented in a candid Wired postmortem), it provided valuable lessons about the possibilities and limitations of participatory news production.

**Status:** Concluded

---

## Themes Across Projects

**Participation:** All of these projects share a focus on breaking down the barrier between journalists and the public. Whether through blog comments, student projects, or crowdsourced reporting, Rosen's building work consistently explores how to make journalism more participatory.

**Experimentation:** Rosen treats building as a form of research—testing ideas in practice to see what works. Projects like NewAssignment.Net and Assignment Zero were explicitly framed as experiments, with lessons learned applied to future work.

**Education:** From Studio 20 to PressThink's role as a teaching platform, Rosen's projects often have an educational dimension, training the next generation of journalists and helping practitioners think differently about their work.
