# Concept Glossary: "The Impossible Press"

**Status:** COMPLETE

---

## Purpose

This glossary defines key terms and concepts introduced or developed in Jay Rosen's 1986 dissertation, with cross-references to how these ideas evolved in his later work.

---

## Core Dissertation Concepts

### The Impossible Press
**Definition:** The fundamental contradiction at the heart of modern journalism: the press is structurally incapable of fulfilling the democratic functions assigned to it because the commercial conditions that enable journalism to thrive are the same conditions that undermine informed public discourse.

**First Appearance:** Chapter 6 (title chapter)

**Key Passage:** "The goal of the modern newspaper was simply to make and sell a product. That the sacred functions of the press had been profaned by commercial success was certainly on the mind of the critic who observed in 1893 that the 'fundamental principle of metropolitan journalism today is to buy white paper at three cents a pound and sell it at ten cents a pound.'"

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Develops into critique of "the professional attitude" in journalism
- Informs analysis of structural vs. individual problems in press criticism
- Foundation for PressThink's approach to media analysis

---

### The Ecological View of Press and Public
**Definition:** An approach to understanding journalism that examines not just the content of news, but the entire symbolic environment in which communication takes place—including the size of the world to be informed about, the social bonds among the audience, the structure of mass media, and the nature of symbols themselves.

**First Appearance:** Conclusion

**Key Passage:** "Journalism needs to be thought of, not as something journalists do, but as a transaction between producers and consumers, writers and readers, the press and the public. And this transaction must be viewed ecologically. That is, it must be set in a particular social context where the world one is to be informed about is symbolized and summed-up, arranged and distorted by a number of communication media."

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Foundation for systems-level analysis of media
- Informs PressThink's holistic approach to press criticism

---

### Attention Granted vs. Attention Gained
**Definition:** The shift from a public realm where citizens voluntarily grant attention to common concerns (as in a town meeting) to one where communicators must compete to capture attention (as in mass media). This shift transforms public discourse from deliberation to performance.

**First Appearance:** Chapter 6

**Key Passage:** "In a traditional public realm—the Greek polis, the town meeting, the dry goods store before mass merchandising—attention is granted, while in the new public realm created by the mass media attention has to be gained. The result is a certain eclipse of the content of public life."

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Prefigures contemporary "attention economy" analysis
- Informs critique of engagement-driven journalism
- Foundation for analysis of clickbait and algorithmic amplification

---

### The Omnicompetent Citizen
**Definition:** The democratic myth that citizens can and should be informed about all matters of public concern—a standard that is impossible to meet in a complex, large-scale society, yet continues to serve as the implicit justification for journalism's functions.

**First Appearance:** Chapter 7 (title: "The Myth of the Omnicompetent Citizen")

**Key Passage:** Draws from Walter Lippmann's critique in *Public Opinion* that the ideal of the "omnicompetent citizen" who can understand and judge all public affairs is a "false ideal" that sets democracy up for failure.

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Connects to "The People Formerly Known as the Audience" (2006)
- Informs critique of journalism's assumptions about its audience
- Foundation for participatory journalism concepts

---

### Communication Without Community
**Definition:** The condition in which media technologies connect people across distance without creating genuine community—where communication exists but shared understanding and common purpose do not.

**First Appearance:** Chapter 5

**Key Passage:** "Sensationalism is what happens when communication outruns the sense of community."

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Anticipates analysis of digital "communities" and parasocial relationships
- Informs critique of social media's false promises of connection

---

### The Professional Attitude
**Definition:** The belief among journalists that adherence to professional standards (accuracy, fairness, completeness) is sufficient to fulfill journalism's democratic functions. Rosen argues this attitude obscures the structural conditions that prevent information from functioning as information.

**First Appearance:** Chapter 6, Conclusion

**Key Passage:** "The most important finding of this study is that the professional attitude is a very limited view of press and public. It assumes too many things and ignores others completely."

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Develops into "The View from Nowhere" critique
- Foundation for analysis of objectivity as ideology
- Informs critique of "he said/she said" journalism

---

### Journalism as Transaction
**Definition:** The understanding that journalism is not simply something journalists do, but a relationship between producers and consumers that takes place within a specific social and symbolic environment.

**First Appearance:** Introduction

**Key Passage:** "Journalism needs to be thought of, not as something journalists do, but as a transaction between producers and consumers, writers and readers, the press and the public."

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Influences analysis of audience as active participants
- Foundation for public journalism movement
- Informs concept of "the people formerly known as the audience"

---

### The Public Realm
**Definition:** The arena in which democratic deliberation takes place. Rosen traces how the public realm has been transformed from physical spaces of face-to-face encounter to symbolic spaces constituted by media—with significant consequences for the quality of public discourse.

**First Appearance:** Throughout, especially Introduction and Chapter 6

**Key Passage:** "Prior to the growth of the press, public discussion always involved a physical location and face to face contact among citizens engaged in speech. For the Greeks this was all there was to 'the public'—a place where people talked and listened."

**Evolution in Later Work:**
- Foundation for analysis of digital public spheres
- Informs PressThink's concern with civic life

## Concepts That Evolved into Later Work

### "View from Nowhere" Precursors
**Dissertation Foundation:** The critique of "the professional attitude"—the belief that journalists can and should stand outside the conflicts they cover, providing neutral information to citizens who will then make informed decisions.

**Key Dissertation Passages:**
- Discussion of objectivity as relation, not property: "Objectivity is not a statement about journalism or the journalist, but a relation, a state of affairs, that may or may not exist among the press, the mind of the reader and the external world."
- Critique of "whig interpretation" of journalism history

**Later Development:**
- "The View from Nowhere" becomes signature concept
- First major articulation: PressThink, 2003-2010
- Critique of "he said/she said" journalism

---

### "The People Formerly Known as the Audience" Precursors
**Dissertation Foundation:** Analysis of how the audience is "assembled" by media rather than pre-existing as a community; critique of the passive, atomized audience model.

**Key Dissertation Passages:**
- On "public transmission, private reception"
- On mass communication creating "a reading and listening audience" rather than "a reading public"

**Later Development:**
- "The People Formerly Known as the Audience" (2006)
- Analysis of participatory media
- Critique of one-to-many broadcast model

---

### Public Journalism Movement Foundations
**Dissertation Foundation:** The Deweyan concern with whether "the public" can be reconstituted as an active democratic agent; critique of press that merely provides information without supporting public formation.

**Key Dissertation Passages:**
- Extensive engagement with John Dewey's *The Public and Its Problems*
- Analysis of how "citizens do not form a public until they collectively understand the problems which concern them"

**Later Development:**
- Active involvement in public journalism movement (1990s)
- *What Are Journalists For?* (1999)
- Studio 20 pedagogical experiments

## Intellectual Influences Cited

### Walter Lippmann
**Referenced in:** Chapters 2, 6, 7, Conclusion

**Key Ideas:**
- Critique of the "omnicompetent citizen" myth
- Analysis of stereotypes in public opinion formation
- Distinction between world and "pictures in our heads"

**Rosen's Engagement:** Lippmann serves as a key interlocutor. Rosen draws on Lippmann's skepticism about democratic competence while departing from Lippmann's technocratic solutions.

---

### John Dewey
**Referenced in:** Chapters 1, 5, Conclusion

**Key Ideas:**
- The public as something that must be "formed," not just informed
- Democracy as communication
- Critique of inherited political categories in changed conditions

**Rosen's Engagement:** Dewey is the dissertation's primary inspiration. Rosen adopts Dewey's view that "citizens do not form a public until they collectively understand the problems which concern them as citizens."

---

### Neil Postman
**Referenced in:** Introduction, Chapters 4, 5, Conclusion

**Key Ideas:**
- Media ecology approach
- Technology as epistemology
- Distinction between typography and television as modes of discourse

**Influence on Dissertation:** As dissertation advisor and intellectual mentor, Postman's influence pervades the work. The ecological approach, concern with media forms, and skepticism about technological progress all reflect Postman's teaching.

---

### James Carey
**Referenced in:** Chapters 4, 5, Conclusion

**Key Ideas:**
- Ritual vs. transmission view of communication
- "The eclipse of the public"
- Communication as culture

**Rosen's Engagement:** Carey provides key vocabulary and framing for understanding the transformation of public life through media.

---

### Hannah Arendt
**Referenced in:** Chapters 4, 5

**Key Ideas:**
- The public realm as space of appearance
- Distinction between public and private
- Decline of public life in modernity

**Rosen's Engagement:** Arendt provides theoretical grounding for understanding what has been lost in the transformation of public life.

---

### Harold Innis
**Referenced in:** Chapter 3, Conclusion

**Key Ideas:**
- Bias of communication
- Space-binding vs. time-binding media
- Empire and communications

**Rosen's Engagement:** Innis provides historical framework for understanding how communication technologies shape social organization.
