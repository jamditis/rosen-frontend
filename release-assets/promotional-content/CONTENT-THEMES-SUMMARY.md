# Content Themes Summary
## Jay Rosen Digital Archive Promotional Strategy

---

## CORE NARRATIVE THEMES

### Theme 1: The Prescient Dissertation

**Core Message:** A 1986 dissertation predicted today's media crisis. Now you can read it.

**Supporting Points:**
- "The phrase 'the press informs the public' obscures more than it reveals" (1986)
- Written before the internet, before social media, before the current crisis
- The Impossible Press argued structural problems couldn't be solved by professional standards
- 40 years later, we're living that prediction

**Use For:** Academic outreach, journalism education, historical context pieces

**Sample Copy:**
> "In 1986, before the World Wide Web existed, Jay Rosen wrote a dissertation that predicted journalism's current crisis. Now you can read it—with 9 interactive tools to explore how prescient it was."

---

### Theme 2: From Archive to Research Platform

**Core Message:** This isn't a museum. It's a knowledge graph you can explore.

**Supporting Points:**
- 25,972 entities connected through 16,539 relationships
- Search across 40 years of journalism criticism
- Trace how concepts evolved over decades
- Identify patterns invisible in isolated pieces

**Use For:** Digital humanities audiences, researchers, data journalists

**Sample Copy:**
> "869 records. 29,187 social posts. 25,972 entities. 16,539 relationships. The Jay Rosen Digital Archive isn't a collection—it's a knowledge graph of four decades of journalism criticism."

---

### Theme 3: Human-AI Collaboration

**Core Message:** 49% AI, 51% human. Acceleration from machines, direction from humans.

**Supporting Points:**
- 275 commits: 140 human, 85 Claude, 50 Copilot
- AI accelerated entity extraction (7 hours → 91 minutes)
- Humans made architecture, design, and editorial decisions
- The future of building is collaborative, not replacement

**Use For:** Tech audiences, AI discourse, future-of-work discussions

**Sample Copy:**
> "Nearly half the commits came from AI assistants. But the decision to reject 'AI slop' aesthetics and design like a 1980s library? That was human. Acceleration vs. direction."

---

### Theme 4: Builder, Not Just Critic

**Core Message:** Data reveals Rosen as infrastructure-builder, not just commentator.

**Supporting Points:**
- 59% of concept adoption comes from individuals (grassroots)
- Founded organizations and projects, not just critiqued media
- Interdisciplinary network: journalists + philosophers + technologists
- "The people formerly known as the audience" = most prominent concept (10/10)

**Use For:** Journalism studies, media criticism, thought leadership pieces

**Sample Copy:**
> "The data reveals something unexpected: Jay Rosen isn't just a critic. He's built infrastructure for alternative journalism. 59% of his concept adoption is grassroots—individuals adopting ideas, not institutions."

---

### Theme 5: Designed for Discovery

**Core Message:** We rejected generic AI aesthetics for distinctive library vibes.

**Supporting Points:**
- Pivoted from "AI slop" to 1980s research library aesthetic
- Aged paper, manila folders, faded ink, card catalog greens
- 200+ CSS design tokens for consistent visual language
- The archive feels like walking into a library, not a startup

**Use For:** Design audiences, UX discussions, authenticity messaging

**Sample Copy:**
> "We got feedback that our design looked like 'AI slop.' So we threw it out. The new aesthetic: 1980s university library. Aged paper. Manila folders. The archive should feel like discovery, not consumption."

---

### Theme 6: Built to Last

**Core Message:** Zero-build deployment means this archive outlives its dependencies.

**Supporting Points:**
- No npm, no Webpack, no Node.js required
- React via CDN (esm.sh)
- FTP deployment to WordPress
- Dependencies rot; static files don't

**Use For:** Tech audiences, digital preservation discussions, open source

**Sample Copy:**
> "This archive is deployable via FTP. No build step. No node_modules. React loaded from CDN. It's counterintuitive in 2025—but archives need to work in 2045. We built for longevity."
