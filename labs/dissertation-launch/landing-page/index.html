Why This Dissertation Matters

The Central Question

What does it really mean when we say "the press informs the public"? Rosen argues this foundational phrase obscures far more than it reveals—and understanding why is essential to grasping journalism's democratic role.

Ahead of Its Time

Written in 1986, this dissertation anticipated many of today's debates about media, democracy, and public life—before the internet, social media, or the current crisis of journalism.

An Ecological View

Rather than focusing on individual journalists or news organizations, Rosen proposes understanding the press-public relationship as an ecosystem shaped by technology, economics, and culture.

About Jay Rosen

Jay Rosen

Professor of Journalism, New York University

Jay Rosen has been on the faculty at NYU since 1986, the same year he completed this dissertation under Neil Postman's supervision. He is one of the country's leading press critics and has written extensively about the relationship between journalism and democracy.

His blog, PressThink, has been exploring these themes since 2003. He was a key figure in the public journalism movement of the 1990s, which drew directly on ideas developed in this dissertation.
