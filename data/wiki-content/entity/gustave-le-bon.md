# Gustave Le Bon

## Who he is

Gustave Le Bon is one of the late nineteenth century theorists Rosen draws on in Chapter 4, where the mass circulation newspaper creates a new kind of social body that is neither a traditional community nor merely a crowd. Le Bon's best-known argument is that individuals in crowds lose their rational faculties and become susceptible to suggestion and emotion, which is the contribution Rosen cites him for.

## In the dissertation

Rosen places Le Bon among the social theorists alarmed by the ambiguous properties of the new public formed by the newspaper. In Chapter 4 (pp. 117-125), Le Bon's crowd stands for the worry that mass society produces irrational crowds open to manipulation and contagion. Rosen uses this against the wider point of the chapter: as a way of tying people together the newspaper has a dissolving as well as a connecting tendency, weakening local ties while creating abstract, unstable bonds.

## From The Impossible Press

"As a way of tying people together the newspaper has a dissolving as well as a connecting tendency." (Chapter 4, p. 117)
