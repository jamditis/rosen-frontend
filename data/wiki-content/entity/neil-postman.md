# Neil Postman

## Who he is

The archive records Neil Postman as a media critic at New York University. The dissertation was completed in the media ecology program he chaired, and its closing argument carries that influence: Rosen proposes an ecological understanding of the relationship between press and public, in which press and public are part of a larger system and a change in one element affects the others. This is the single idea that explains why Rosen leans on Postman, so the page keeps to it.

## In the dissertation

Rosen builds toward an ecological view of the press-public relationship. In the conclusion (Conclusion, p. 374) he identifies five factors that must be inserted between "press" and "public" for an adequate understanding: the size and scale of the world to be informed about, the character of the social bond among audience members, structural characteristics of mass media, the nature of language and symbols, and the professional attitude. His most important finding is that the professional attitude is a very limited view of press and public. The ecological framing itself (p. 400) treats press and public as part of a larger system, so that changes in one element affect all the others.

## From The Impossible Press

"For any press anywhere, making things public does not a public make." (Conclusion, p. 400)
