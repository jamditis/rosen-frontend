# Gabriel Tarde

## Who he is

Rosen cites Gabriel Tarde as one of the late nineteenth century theorists who tried to make sense of the new kind of social body created by the mass circulation newspaper. Tarde is best known for distinguishing the public from the crowd: a group dispersed in space yet connected by shared attention to the same media rather than by physical proximity. Rosen uses this distinction to describe the public as the social body that emerges from the newspaper form.

## In the dissertation

In Chapter 4, Rosen argues that the mass circulation newspaper created a social body that was neither a traditional community nor merely a crowd, with ambiguous properties that alarmed social theorists (Chapter 4, p. 117). Against the crowd psychology that worried his contemporaries, Tarde drew a line between the crowd and the public, the dispersed group held together by common attention to the same media rather than by being physically gathered (Chapter 4, pp. 126-134). This sets up Rosen's claim that the newspaper, as a way of tying people together, both connects and dissolves.

## From The Impossible Press

"As a way of tying people together the newspaper has a dissolving as well as a connecting tendency." (Chapter 4, p. 117)
