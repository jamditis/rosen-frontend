# The impossibility of the informed citizen

## The idea

Democratic theory imagines a citizen who is generally competent and naturally informed about public affairs. Rosen argues that the scale and complexity of modern life make that ideal impossible to reach. No amount of reporting can turn every citizen into an expert on everything the modern state does, so the press cannot deliver the informed public that democratic theory assumes.

## From The Impossible Press

One could free all the facts in the world and still not be informing the public, for the important question was not what was released into the environment, but what took shape in the beliefs and behavior of the average citizen. (Chapter 7, p. 280)
