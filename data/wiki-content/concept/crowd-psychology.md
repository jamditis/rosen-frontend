# Crowd psychology

## The idea

Toward the end of the nineteenth century, social theorists grew anxious that mass society was producing irrational crowds, prone to suggestion, manipulation, and emotional contagion. The fear was that as people were gathered into large, dense aggregations, the steadying influence of reason gave way to impulse spread from person to person. Rosen uses this body of thought as the backdrop for the new social body created by the newspaper: when theorists looked at the readers the mass press assembled, they reached for the language of the crowd to describe what they saw.

## Le Bon and Tarde

Two figures anchor the chapter's treatment of this anxiety. Gustave Le Bon argued that individuals in a crowd lose their rational faculties and fall under the sway of suggestion and emotion. Gabriel Tarde drew a distinction between the crowd and the public, describing the public as a dispersed group held together by shared attention to the same media rather than by physical proximity. Between them they mark the move the chapter title names, from crowd to public: the readers of the mass press are not packed together in a square, yet they form a body that earlier writers could only understand by analogy to the crowd.
