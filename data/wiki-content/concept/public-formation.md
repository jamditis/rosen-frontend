# Public formation

## The idea

Rosen argues that the public is not a body that exists in advance, waiting for the news to arrive. It is something the mass media create. The newspaper gathers together people who would otherwise have no connection to one another and gives them a common object of attention. In this sense the press does not merely serve a public that is already there; it forms one. That is also why the public it makes carries the marks of how it was made, abstract and assembled rather than grown out of shared life.

## Dissolving bonds

The act of forming a public has a cost. While the newspaper connects people across distances, it weakens local community bonds by directing attention outward. Readers turn from the people beside them toward events far away, so the same paper that ties strangers together loosens the face-to-face ties that held a town or neighborhood together. This is the dissolving tendency Rosen sets next to the connecting one: the newspaper builds a wide, thin web of attention by drawing energy away from the dense, local web it replaces.

## Unstable publics

Because the bond is shared attention rather than shared experience and obligation, the publics the media form are inherently unstable. They gather around an event and dissolve when attention moves on to the next one. A public assembled this way has no lasting hold on its members; it lacks the steadiness that communities draw from common life and mutual duty. The newspaper can summon a public into being around a story, but it cannot make that public last beyond the story's hold on attention.

## From The Impossible Press

As a way of tying people together the newspaper has a dissolving as well as a connecting tendency. (Chapter 4, p. 140)
