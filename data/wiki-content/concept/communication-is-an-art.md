# Communication is an art, not a science

## The idea

Rosen draws from Dewey the claim that forming a public is an art of communication, not a science of transmission. Sending facts out into the world is not the same as informing people. What matters is whether ideas reach an audience prepared to receive them and whether the energies at work in public life can actually be traced to their consequences. Without that, Dewey warned, what passes for public opinion is opinion only in its weaker sense.

## Cultivation: growing understanding over time

The art Rosen describes works the way cultivation does. Like a farmer, the communicator prepares the soil, plants seeds with care, and nurtures growth over time rather than expecting understanding to appear all at once. Seeds thrown out at random do not take. Understanding has to be tended into being, which is why the sowing-seeds image stands at the center of this part of the dissertation.

## Context: the same message, different effects

Because communication is an art, the same information can inform one person and confuse another depending on how it is presented and received. Context shapes meaning. A fact dropped into the wrong setting lands differently than the same fact placed where an audience can make sense of it. The communicator's task is therefore not only to be accurate but to attend to the conditions under which a message is taken up.

## From The Impossible Press

Unless there are methods for detecting the energies at work and tracing them through an intricate network of interactions to their consequences, what passes for public opinion will be "opinion" only in its derogatory sense. (Chapter 8, p. 360)
