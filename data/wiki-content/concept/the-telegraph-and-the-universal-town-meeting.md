# The telegraph and the universal town meeting

## The idea

When the telegraph arrived it inspired visions of a world united by instant communication, a universal town meeting that could span continents. The newspaper was cast in the same role: a town meeting for politics, gathering a dispersed nation into a single deliberating body. Rosen treats this as the recurring hope attached to each new medium, the belief that faster and wider communication could restore the face-to-face deliberation once possible in a small community. The trouble was that connecting people across great distances did not recreate the community on which a real town meeting depends.

## The recurring pattern

The telegraph was only the first instance of a pattern that repeats with each new medium. Every communication technology follows the same arc: utopian hopes that it will restore small-scale democratic participation, then the realization that technology cannot solve a social problem. The dream returns with each invention because the underlying wish, to close the distance that separates the public from the events that concern it, is never satisfied by speed or reach alone.

## From The Impossible Press

The newspaper constitutes a universal town meeting for politics. (Chapter 3, p. 108)
