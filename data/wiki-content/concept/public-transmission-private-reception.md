# Public transmission, private reception

## The idea

A public message goes out to many people at once, but each person takes it in alone. Unlike the public square, where speech and reply happen face to face, mass media is consumed in private: reading the newspaper or watching television at home, by oneself. Rosen pairs that private reception with a second feature, the one-way flow. The audience can receive but cannot answer back or take part, so the connection runs in a single direction, from a remote source to a private reader. The result is a form of contact that links people to events without linking them to one another.

## Received alone, with no way to answer

Two features of this arrangement reinforce each other. The first is where the message lands: not in a shared space but in the home, where it is read or watched in isolation rather than in the company of fellow citizens. The second is the shape of the channel: mass media creates a one-way flow in which there is no response channel, so the reader cannot reply, correct, or participate. Together they describe a kind of communication that reaches a mass audience while leaving each member of it on their own.

## From The Impossible Press

Our system of communication is not addressed at the public but at private individuals. We have evolved a radical form of mobilized privacy: the individual hooked into long lines of communication from remote sources. (Chapter 5, p. 160)
