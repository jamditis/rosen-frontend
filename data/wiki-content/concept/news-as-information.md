# News as information

## The idea

Rosen argues that news functions as information only when it meets three conditions at once. The reader needs structure, an ordered understanding into which a new fact can fit, because without it facts are meaningless data points. The reader needs a sense of action, a feeling that there is something to be done with what is learned. And the situation needs contingency, an outcome that is still uncertain and depends on decisions not yet made. When any of these is missing, the same item that could have informed the reader instead becomes something to watch rather than act on. This is why information cannot be located inside the news item itself: it lives in the relation between a person and a changing environment.

## Structure: ordered understanding

Information requires a framework of understanding. A fact arriving without a structure to receive it is a meaningless data point. The reader has to already hold an ordered picture of the situation for a new report to add anything to it (pp. 55-58).

## Action: a feeling of participation

News informs only when it connects to potential action. The reader must feel that there is something to do with what is learned, that the report bears on a choice or a course of conduct that is theirs to take (pp. 59-62).

## Contingency: unpredictable outcomes

Information matters when outcomes are uncertain, when the future still depends on decisions yet to be made. Where the result is already settled, fresh facts about it carry no informing power, however dramatic they are (pp. 63-65).

## From The Impossible Press

Information is not really "in" the items which come over the wire and make their way into the newspaper. It is "in" the relations between people and a changing environment. (Chapter 2, p. 58)
