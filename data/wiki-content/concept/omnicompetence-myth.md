# The omnicompetence myth

## The idea

The older democratic theory assumes an omnicompetent citizen, one who can form informed opinions on all public questions. Lippmann called that expectation entirely unworkable, and Rosen builds on the judgment. The press is like the beam of a searchlight that moves restlessly about, bringing one episode and then another out of darkness into vision, and people cannot do the work of the world by that light alone. The image makes the point precise: coverage is intermittent and selective by nature, so it can illuminate episodes but cannot supply the steady, full competence the ideal demands of every citizen.

## Too much to know

One reason the myth fails is complexity. Modern society is too complex for any individual to understand fully. Specialization becomes necessary, and that very necessity opens gaps in public understanding that no general reader can close. The expert knows a narrow field; the citizen cannot become expert in all of them at once. Rosen reads this as a limit on what the press can deliver: it cannot turn a population into specialists on every question simply by reporting more.
