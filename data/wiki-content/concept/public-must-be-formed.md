# A public must be formed, not informed

## The idea

Against the picture of a ready-made public that only lacks the facts, Dewey held that the public itself has to be brought into being. It forms when people come to share an understanding of the problems they hold in common, not when information is delivered to an audience already assembled. Rosen presents this as Dewey's answer to Lippmann: the failure of the informed citizen is real, but the remedy is to create the public, not to hand public affairs to experts.

## Why it must begin locally

For Dewey, the work of forming a public starts where people stand. Public life must begin locally, in the settings where people can experience the consequences of decisions directly and take part in a way that matters to them. A public assembled at that scale has something to act on; one addressed only from a distance does not. This is the ground on which a wider public can later be built.

## From The Impossible Press

Seeds are sown not by being thrown out at random, but by being so distributed as to take root and have a chance of growth. (Chapter 8, p. 345)
