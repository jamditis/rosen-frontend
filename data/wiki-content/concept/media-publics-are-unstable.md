# Media-created publics are inherently unstable

## The idea

A public gathered by mass media forms around an event and scatters when attention moves on. Rosen contrasts these media publics with communities bound by shared experience and obligation: the former lack the stability that sustained democratic action requires. The audience can be assembled in an instant and lost just as fast.

## Why it endures

The dynamic Rosen described has only sharpened online, where publics coalesce around a viral moment and dissolve within days. It explains why mobilizing attention is easy and sustaining a movement is hard, and why outrage cycles rarely convert into durable public life.
