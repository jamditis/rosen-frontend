# Sensationalism

## The idea

Rosen reads sensationalism not as a moral failing of the press or a corrupt taste in readers, but as a structural problem. When events carry no stake for the audience, the audience has no reason to attend, and the only way left to hold them is to raise the emotional pitch. Sensationalism is what news does when it can no longer count on the reader to care. It belongs to news as drama, the kind of news that asks only to be watched, rather than to news as information, the kind that asks to be acted on.

## Human interest: stories without stakes

The human interest story is the clearest case. It engages the emotions without requiring any action, and the reader consumes it as they would a piece of fiction. Nothing in it turns on a choice the reader might make, so it works as drama: the journalist becomes a dramatist and the reader becomes a spectator (pp. 66-75).

## From The Impossible Press

Sensationalism is not a perverse appetite for the crude and spectacular, but the increasing difficulty of interesting a population which does not act on its world. (Chapter 2, p. 80)
