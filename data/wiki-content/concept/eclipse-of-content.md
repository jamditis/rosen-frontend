# The eclipse of content

## The idea

Once attention has to be captured rather than granted, the struggle to be heard starts to shape what the press produces. Rosen describes how the competition for attention elevates presentation over substance, so that what captures attention may not be what informs. Content does not disappear, but it is eclipsed, pushed behind the work of being noticed. The press that must win a place in a crowded field begins to favor the qualities that win attention over the qualities that inform.

## The front page

The front page is where this logic becomes a physical object. It is designed to grab attention in a crowded marketplace, and its headlines, images, and layout serve that commercial function. The page is not arranged to lay out the day's events in order of public importance; it is arranged for impact, to pull a reader toward one paper rather than another. In that sense the front page is the clearest sign of content being eclipsed by the demands of attention.

## From The Impossible Press

When there is an indefinite supply of available information, attention is scarce. The battle for attention is then the very substance of communication. (Chapter 5, p. 180)
