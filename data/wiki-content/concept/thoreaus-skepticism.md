# Thoreau's skepticism about faster communication

## The idea

Against the enthusiasm for the telegraph, Rosen sets the doubt of Henry David Thoreau, who questioned whether faster communication served any purpose at all. Thoreau's point was not that the wire would fail to connect Maine and Texas but that connection is not the same as having something worth saying. The example sharpens the chapter's argument: speed and reach answer the question of how to communicate, while leaving untouched the harder question of whether there is anything to communicate about and whether anyone is in a position to act on it.

## From The Impossible Press

We are in great haste to construct a magnetic telegraph from Maine to Texas; but Maine and Texas, it may be, have nothing important to communicate. (Chapter 3, pp. 110-114)
