# The pseudo-environment

## The idea

Lippmann argued that people do not act on the world as it is. They act on a representation of it, an inner picture assembled from what they have read, heard, and already believed. Rosen takes this seriously as a problem for the press. If what guides behavior is the picture in the head rather than the event itself, then publishing facts is not the same as informing the public. The decisive question is not what was released into the environment but what took shape in the beliefs and behavior of the average citizen. A press that pours more information into the world can still leave the pseudo-environment untouched.

## The reality gap

Between the world and our picture of it lies a gap, and that gap produces systematic misunderstanding rather than occasional error. Rosen stresses that the press cannot simply bridge it. The distance is built into how perception works, so adding coverage does not close it on its own. This is why he treats the gap as a structural condition the press operates inside, not a defect a better newsroom could remove.

## From The Impossible Press

One could free all the facts in the world and still not be informing the public, for the important question was not what was released into the environment, but what took shape in the beliefs and behavior of the average citizen. (Chapter 7, p. 280)
