# Two views of news

## What the chapter argues

Rosen separates two types of news that look alike on the page but do different work. News as information asks the reader to act, and it can only function when three conditions hold: structure, an ordered understanding that makes facts meaningful; action, a feeling that the reader can do something with what is learned; and contingency, outcomes that are still unsettled. News as drama asks for nothing but attention. The human interest story engages emotion without requiring action, so the journalist becomes a dramatist and the audience becomes spectators rather than participants. Read this way, sensationalism is not a taste for the crude but a symptom of the difficulty of holding the interest of a population that does not act on its world.

## From The Impossible Press

Information is not really "in" the items which come over the wire and make their way into the newspaper. It is "in" the relations between people and a changing environment. (Chapter 2, p. 58)
