# From crowd to public

## What the chapter argues

Chapter 4, From Crowd to Public, takes up the social body that the mass circulation newspaper brought into being. Rosen describes it as something new: neither a traditional, face-to-face community nor merely a crowd, but a public with ambiguous properties that alarmed late nineteenth century social theorists. The chapter sets the crowd psychology of that period against the emerging idea of the public, then asks what kind of bond the newspaper actually creates among the strangers it gathers.

## The connecting and dissolving newspaper

The chapter's central claim is that the public is the social body that emerges from the newspaper form, and that this form has two tendencies at once. The newspaper connects people across distance by giving scattered strangers a shared object of attention, and at the same time it dissolves the local ties that once held communities together by directing attention outward. The bonds it creates are abstract and unstable rather than rooted in shared experience and obligation.

## From The Impossible Press

As a way of tying people together the newspaper has a dissolving as well as a connecting tendency. (Chapter 4, p. 140)
