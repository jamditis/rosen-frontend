# Communication without community

## What the chapter argues

Chapter 5 takes up the gap between being connected and being together. The modern public is gathered together without actually meeting, and Rosen treats that as a fact that changes both public life and the function of the press. Our system of communication is not addressed at the public but at private individuals: each person is hooked into long lines of communication that run from remote sources into a private space. Out of this arrangement Rosen draws two linked consequences. First, a radical form of mobilized privacy, in which public messages are received alone and with no channel to answer back. Second, an attention economy, in which the press must capture attention rather than be granted it, and so begins to structure events according to its own demands for drama, conflict, brevity, and consumability.

## From The Impossible Press

Our system of communication is not addressed at the public but at private individuals. We have evolved a radical form of mobilized privacy: the individual hooked into long lines of communication from remote sources. (Chapter 5, p. 160)
