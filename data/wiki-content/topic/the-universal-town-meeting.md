# The universal town meeting

## What the chapter argues

Chapter 3 examines the dream that each new communication technology will restore the scale of democratic participation once possible in small communities. The telegraph and the penny press inspired utopian visions of democracy restored, a universal town meeting that could span continents. Rosen argues that these visions ignored a basic problem: extending communication across distances where no genuine community existed did not recreate the conditions of the town meeting. Each new medium repeated the same arc of hope followed by disappointment, and the deeper paradox was that better communication made the task of informing the public harder rather than easier, because it ensured there would always be more to communicate about.

## From The Impossible Press

Improvements in communication also make communication more difficult because they ensure that there will be more to communicate about. (Chapter 3, p. 95)
