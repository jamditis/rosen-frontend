# The impossible press

## What the chapter argues

This is the titular chapter of the dissertation. Rosen argues that the professionalization of journalism after 1900 was an attempt to reconcile a contradiction: journalism is a business, and journalism claims to be a public service. The professional attitude that grew out of this effort obscured fundamental problems in the relationship between the press and the public. Yellow journalism had put a sharper edge on the conflict between private profits and public duties, and professional standards such as objectivity, accuracy, fairness, and completeness became the way journalists tried to prove their public responsibility. But these standards assume too much. They take for granted that a public already exists and is ready to be informed, and so they leave the harder question, what a public is and how one is formed, untouched.

## The impossible press

The press that emerges from this history is impossible in a specific sense: it tries to solve the whole problem of public life by controlling the conduct of journalists. A failing rooted in the structure of modern public life is treated as if it were a matter of individual behavior. Better journalists, more careful and more fair, cannot create a public where none exists, which is why Rosen calls the result an impossible press.

## From The Impossible Press

An impossible press was born, one which sought to solve the whole problem of public life simply by controlling the conduct of journalists. (Chapter 6, p. 220)
